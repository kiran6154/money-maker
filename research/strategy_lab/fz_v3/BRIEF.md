## Protocol (pre-registered before any number is looked at)
- IS (learning / hypothesis) = sessions 2021-10-01 .. 2025-12-31. OOS (confirmation, touched once at the end) = 2026-01-01 .. 2026-09-25. Nothing chosen on OOS. Report both, always with the session-matched random control (fz_report.random_control) and the kept-vs-refused permutation p.
- Unit of analysis = Foundation SETUP with its own Foundation trade outcome (pts, net at 65 lot, exit reason) from engine.run with the ST1 rules on 1m (touch, prev_swing) and ST2 rules on 5m (touch, choch_candle). A "gate" is any rule that turns a SETUP into skip; a gate is judged by: net of kept, net of skipped, share of winners skipped (recall of losers vs precision), and the control percentile.
- Every candidate rule must be expressible as a compact config (thresholds, a rule list of at most 8 conjunctive rules of depth <= 3), because the lab forbids trading numbers in code; provenance = "learned on IS 2021-10..2025-12, script <name>, sha".
- Costs: charges + slippage = 1,114 INR per 1m trade; any gate that trades less raises net. Selection = kept-vs-skipped expectancy difference and the control percentile, never net alone.
- Look-ahead: every feature at a SETUP bar uses bars <= that bar only; forward returns are labels, never features. State it per feature.

## Hypotheses to test (each an agent)
H1 Gate audit: what do ST7/ST8 (rooms, frozen rules) and ST5/ST6 skip? Split skipped SETUPs by Foundation outcome (win/loss, pts buckets), by hour bin, by read, by block reason. Does the gate skip losers more than winners (precision/recall)? Where does it skip the big winners (the "late runners" the user saw)? Same on IS and OOS.
H2 Sideways = CHoCH without break: define regime per bar from engine events: n_choch_since_bos (CHoCHs since the last BOS), n_bos_since_choch, bars_since_choch, alternation count in the last K events, and the range of the last N bars vs ATR. Foundation P&L by regime, per timeframe, per hour. Does "CHoCH, CHoCH, no BOS" mark losing trades? Test a regime filter (skip SETUPs when n_choch_since_bos >= k) on IS, confirm on OOS, with control percentile.
H3 Visibly high volume: define a bar as visibly high volume when volume >= v x median volume of the last N bars (grid v in 2,3,4; N in 20,60), same session. Measure price reaction after such bars (forward 5/15/30 bar returns and MAE/MFE, direction relative to the bar's close vs open), whether the bar's high/low holds for the next N bars, and Foundation outcomes for SETUPs within M bars after a high-volume bar, by direction agreement.
H4 Levels respected or broken: for protected levels and room edges (ST7 rooms card) and swing highs/lows: at each touch, measure follow-through: break (close beyond by > x ATR within N bars) vs retest/respect (return inside). Distribution of the move size after a confirmed break vs after a respect. Number of retests before a break. Foundation SETUPs taken at a respected level vs at a broken level: outcomes. Is "retests, retests, respects" measurable and does it predict?
H5 Learned gate (offline contextual bandit / supervised): build the per-SETUP feature table (card fields from fz.run with ST7/ST8 config, regime features from H2, volume features from H3, level context from H4, hour, gap, session_bar, direction, ATR, Foundation SL distance). Label = Foundation net > 0 (and pts). On IS: (a) a depth-<=3 decision tree and a greedy rule-list search (exhaustive single/pair thresholds) for rules that skip losers with minimal winner loss; (b) a gradient boosting reference (sklearn is installed: 1.7.2) for the ceiling. Choose by IS cross-validation only. Evaluate once on OOS: kept net vs raw vs random control, precision/recall on losers, calibration. Output the winning rule list as JSON config candidate for ST13/ST14. State why sequential RL is not justified (one-step decision, ~2k-6k SETUPs) and how this is a contextual bandit.

## Addendum (2026-09-29, user instructions after the first launch)

1. **"Ensure to use latest AI and machine-learning techniques to understand the patterns and come up with how to trade in this."** The classical H1-H5 program above is the floor, not the ceiling. A design panel (three lenses: quant-ML canon, sequence/representation learning, decision-making/RL; two judges) selected the modern studies; its output is `DESIGN_PANEL.md` next to this file and is part of the program. The final deliverable is a trading playbook: which Foundation SETUPs to take or skip by regime / time / volume / level context, how to size, how to exit, with out-of-sample evidence and the random control.
2. **"You can use it to figure out the patterns and code in this as well."** Survivors are coded into the lab as new strategies ST13 (1 min) and ST14 (5 min) (`strategies/strategy_13.json`, `strategy_14.json`, reserved and confirmed free with the other sessions on 2026-09-29; list the folder again before creating them). ST5-ST8 stay frozen; nothing in `engine.py` changes; no trading number in code (every threshold, weight file path and learner hyper-parameter is a strategy-file key with a `source`).
3. **"Ensure not to take shortcuts or skip best analysis or modelling or training due to hardware constraint; if that's the call then move to Claude cloud by committing required files."** The four input files are committed under `fz_v3/data/` for that reason. In the cloud: install what the program needs (xgboost, shap, torch, hmmlearn, stumpy, statsmodels, mapie ... as the design calls for), use every core, and run the full grids, bootstraps and training schedules the design specifies; never shrink a model or a resample count to fit a box.
4. **"Even incorporate journal every trade and induce reinforcement learning after each trade to optimize strategy, money management and profit factor."** Two requirements:
   - **Trade journal.** One row per Foundation SETUP (taken or skipped) and per closed trade: timestamp, direction, the full feature vector at the decision bar (card fields, regime, volume, level context, time), the policy's decision and its stated reason, the policy's state id (which model / which update it was made with), fill, exit reason, MFE / MAE, pts, gross, costs, net, R-multiple, and the running book (equity, drawdown, rolling profit factor). This journal is the training set of the online learner and the audit trail of every decision. In the lab build it is a table the strategy writes (`fz_journal.csv` or a `journal` block in the results), never a side effect hidden in code.
   - **Learning after each trade (online / walk-forward).** The policy used at trade t is fitted only on journal rows of trades closed before t (strictly causal; anchored or rolling window as a config key), and is refitted after every closed trade (or every k trades, k a key). Actions: take / skip and position size (lots, bounded by a money-management rule such as fractional Kelly on the calibrated win probability and the expected R, with a maximum lots key and a daily loss stop key). Reward: net P&L per trade with the objective reported as net, profit factor (gross wins / gross losses), max drawdown and the kept-vs-skipped expectancy; because profit factor is not additive, the learner optimises a per-trade surrogate (net, or net with an asymmetric loss penalty) and the policy is *selected* on walk-forward profit factor. Candidate learners, all to be run and compared: contextual bandits with exploration (LinUCB / Thompson sampling on a logistic or tree-embedded model), online gradient-boosted refits, and an offline-RL exit policy (fitted Q-iteration / conservative Q-learning over bar-by-bar in-trade states with doubly-robust off-policy evaluation). Evaluate the whole walk-forward path on IS (learning curve: after how many trades does the policy beat "take everything" and the random control?) and then once on OOS with the learner continuing to update through OOS exactly as it would live. Compare against the static IS-fitted model and the frozen ST7/ST8 gate.
   - The lab strategy that results carries the learner as config (algorithm, features, update cadence, priors, sizing keys, provenance), with a **deterministic, replayable** update path from the journal, so that a rerun over the same tape reproduces the same decisions bit for bit.
